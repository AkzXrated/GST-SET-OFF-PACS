The 6 Golden Rules — Everything Else Follows From These
1️⃣
Bank splits GST at entry time
Every Bank purchase: half the GST goes into the purchase/expense head (permanently absorbed), half into SA GST I/P (0200). No month-end reversal exists.
2️⃣
Neethi & Depot book full GST
100% of invoice GST goes to their SA GST I/P heads. Depot bills must be entered in PacScore same day AND given to CA.
3️⃣
RCM is always cash, closed mid-month
RCM can never be paid by ITC. Pay challan before 20th, route it immediately (Dr SL O/P / Cr 4445). By month-end, RCM is already fully closed.
4️⃣
Two set-off entries close the month
Entry 1: Dr SL O/P / Cr SA I/P = ITC used ①. Entry 2 (only if regular cash paid): Dr SL O/P / Cr 4445 = cash ②. No third RCM entry — ever.
5️⃣
Numbers come from GSTR-3B, not ledgers
Read ① and ② from the filed GSTR-3B PDF — Table 6.1 Section (A): ITC column = ①, cash column = ②. Skip the website summary screen; electronic ledgers are for verification only.
6️⃣
After set-off, heads read clean
SL O/P = ₹0 for the month. SA I/P = carry-forward ITC (normal for Depot). 4445 = ₹0. Books mirror the portal exactly.

The Three Players
👤
YOU — the bookkeeper
You live in PacScore. Enter bills and sales, pay the challans, pass the month-end entries, run the health check. You never touch a government form.
🧑‍💼
YOUR CA — the filer
Takes the data you send by the 8th and turns it into filed returns (GSTR-1 by the 11th, GSTR-3B by the 20th). Tells you if a cash challan is needed.
🏛️
GST PORTAL — the referee
The government website (gst.gov.in). Whatever is filed there is the truth — when your books and the portal disagree, the portal wins. Always.
The Paperwork, Translated to Plain Words
GSTR-1
"Here's what we sold this month" — your sales report
Who makes it: the CA, from the sales register YOU send. Your job: send accurate sales + exempt-income totals by the 8th. You never open this return yourself.
GSTR-2B
"Here's what suppliers say they sold YOU" — your purchase statement
Auto-generated by the portal on the 14th from every supplier's GSTR-1. It is the official list of ITC you're allowed to claim. Your job: nothing monthly — open it only when ITC looks short (a missing bill here means the supplier didn't upload it).
GSTR-3B ⭐
"The monthly bill & how it was paid" — THE one you read
Filed by the CA by the 20th. Shows total output tax, how much was paid using ITC (that's your ①) and how much in cash (gives your ②). Your job: open it once a month, read two numbers, pass two entries. This is the only return you ever need to understand.
CASH LEDGER
Electronic Cash Ledger — your wallet AT the government
Challan money lands here first, then gets used to pay tax. Your job: nothing — cross-check only if a payment seems lost.
CREDIT LEDGER
Electronic Credit Ledger — your ITC passbook
Every month: a credit (ITC earned) and a debit (ITC used). Its closing balance is what your SA GST I/P head must match in the health check. Also the 10-minute year-end verifier: sum all its debit rows = your year's total set-off.
CHALLAN
A deposit slip for the government wallet
The only thing you ever "do" on the portal: create it, pay via net-banking, save the PDF. Its receipt number is the CPIN — put it in your narration.
🏦
One analogy that holds it all together: you hold an account AT the government. The Cash Ledger is that account, a challan is the deposit slip, the Credit Ledger is your rewards-points balance, GSTR-3B is the monthly account statement, and the CA is the branch manager who does the paperwork for you.

Map 2 · Where the Money Actually Flows
MONEY IN — SALES
Customer pays you price + GST
The GST part is not your income — you're holding it for the government. It piles up in SL GST Output Tax (liability: "we owe govt").
MONEY OUT — PURCHASES
You pay supplier price + GST
That GST is refundable credit (ITC). It piles up in SA GST Input Tax (asset: "govt owes us"). Bank keeps only half (Rule 38); Neethi/Depot keep all.
↘    ↙
MONTH END — SET-OFF (ENTRY ①)
The two piles cancel each other
No real money moves. Dr SL O/P / Cr SA I/P for the ① amount shown on the GSTR-3B.
↙    ↘
IF OUTPUT WAS BIGGER
Pay the difference by challan — Entry ②
Real money leaves the bank: Dr SL O/P / Cr 4445 (plus Dr 4445 / Cr Bank on payment day).
IF ITC WAS BIGGER
Leftover carries forward — do nothing
Stays as a Dr balance in SA I/P, matching the portal Credit Ledger. Completely normal for the Depot.
🏠
RCM takes a separate shortcut road: rent GST never joins this cancelling game — it must be paid in cash mid-month (challan by the 18th). Its ITC then joins the SA pile like any other credit.

The Portal Website Screen — Skip It (For Now)
🖥️
The website’s summary screen shows different cash numbers than the PDF — for the same return. Its “Paid through Cash” adds regular cash AND RCM cash into one figure (April: 0 + 3,114 shows as ₹3,114). Nothing is wrong — it just lumps what the PDF keeps separate. Beginner rule: always download the PDF and read only Section (A). If someday you must read the screen: “Paid through Credit” = ① as-is, and “Paid through Cash” − the actual RCM challan amount = ②.

📗 Electronic Credit Ledger

The 5-Minute Monthly Audit — Do This Every Month, Not Just at Year-End
CHECK 1
Taxable value: your register vs the filed return
Run your PacScore "GST Headwise Receipts Summary" for the month and compare its total taxable value + GST to GSTR-1 Table 3.1(a)/3.1.1. These should match exactly, or you should know precisely why not (a genuine late entry, a credit note). April 2026 matched to the paisa — that's the proof this check works. June 2026 didn't (₹9,100 short) — that's the proof this check catches real problems.
CHECK 2
ITC claimed vs GSTR-2B — never more than 2B shows
GSTR-3B Table 4A "All other ITC" must not exceed what GSTR-2B lists as available for that month. If it's higher, that's a Rule 36(4) variance (a supplier filed late, or wrong invoices were claimed) — the portal already flags this; don't just note it, ask which invoice and confirm it gets trued up later.
CHECK 3
Bank only — Rule 38 reversal = exactly 50% of gross ITC
Table 4B(1) reversal ÷ Table 4A total should be almost exactly 0.50 (including RCM ITC — confirmed from your own April 2026 filing, ₹18,629.96 reversal on the month's gross ITC). If reversal is missing, zero, or not close to half, that reversal did not happen — see the real example below, from your own CA's file.
CHECK 4
RCM value matches your known fixed rent
Table 3.1(d) inward RCM taxable value × 18% should equal Bank ₹3,114 / Neethi ₹900 / Depot ₹694 (or whatever the current rent implies). A drifting figure with no rent change is worth a question — this is exactly how the Kunnoth Theru ₹8,800-vs-₹6,900 rent conflict surfaced.
CHECK 5
Table 6.1 arithmetic closes
Net Tax Payable = your Check 1 output. ITC-paid (①) + Cash-paid (②) should sum to Net Tax Payable for the (A) row, exactly. If the CA's Section (A) doesn't add up on the return itself, that's a red flag before you even compare to your books.

Do I need a separate set-off entry for RCM?▼
No — ever. RCM is legally cash-only (ITC cannot pay it), so it never waits for month-end set-off. The mid-month routing entry (Dr SL O/P / Cr 4445 at challan time) closes it completely. The RCM ITC it generates just joins your normal SA I/P pool and gets consumed through ordinary Entry 1. If SL O/P shows a leftover after Entry 1 + 2, the likeliest cause is a missed routing entry from some month — find it and pass it.
Why doesn't the Bank have a 50% reversal entry at month-end?▼
Because the 50% split happens at entry time: half of every invoice's GST is absorbed into the purchase/expense head, only the eligible half touches 0200. The books never hold ineligible ITC, so there's nothing to reverse. (Other banks book 100% then reverse 50% as a second entry — same final position, just a different audit-trail style. Your CA's GSTR-3B does show the gross-then-reverse presentation in Table 4, but that's the return's format, not your books' problem.)
Table 6.1 or Electronic Ledgers — which is the source of truth for ① and ②?▼
The filed GSTR-3B (Table 6.1 / portal 6.1 summary). It's a per-month statement designed exactly for this. Ledgers are running annual accounts — fine for verifying afterwards (Credit Ledger debit on filing date = ①) and for the year-in-one-file download, but reading set-off amounts from them invites month-mixing errors.
Portal shows only "Paid through Cash / Credit" — where are Sections A and B?▼
The online summary collapses them. Recover the split yourself: ① = Paid through Credit (RCM can't be paid by credit, so it's pure regular ITC) and ② = Paid through Cash − known RCM (Bank 3,114 · Neethi 900 · Depot 694). The downloadable PDF still shows A/B explicitly if you prefer it.
Auditor asks: why did "GST Paid to Govt" move from Contingencies to Adjusting Heads — Due To or Due By?▼
Due By. Script: "This head records completed challan payments awaiting adjustment against output liability — a clearing account, not an uncertain obligation, so Contingencies was a misfit. It carries a debit balance (cash out, pending matching), which under our pro forma is Adjusting Heads — Due By. With the monthly set-off process now in place the balance stays near ₹0, confirming its transient clearing nature." Have the current 4445 balance ready as the concrete number.
The return shows a Rule 36(4) variance warning — what do I do?▼
It means ITC claimed exceeded what GSTR-2B auto-populated — usually a supplier filed late. Ask CA: which invoice, and was it trued-up in a later month? Nothing changes in your journal entries, but keep the CA's answer on file — unexplained upward variance is a standard audit flag. (Your Depot April 2026 return carries this warning.)
Some banks say they only use the Electronic Credit Ledger and no GST heads at all — is that wrong?▼
It's a valid simpler alternative: absorb all GST into cost, keep one payable head, let the portal be the ITC record. You chose the detailed method (separate I/P and O/P heads), which gives better in-book visibility — but only works if the set-off entries are actually passed monthly. Doing the detailed setup without set-off (the old situation) is the worst of both worlds.
SL O/P still has a balance after both entries — what's wrong?▼
Trace in this order: (1) a month's RCM routing missed — residue will be a multiple of 3,114/900/694; (2) sales in books CA never filed — compare book output vs return 3.1; (3) SGST half missing on output entries — residue ≈ half of expected (the FY 26-27 HO issue); (4) branch output not consolidated. The residue amount itself usually identifies the cause.
How do I pull a whole year of portal data at once?▼
Three ways: (1) Services → Ledgers → Electronic Cash/Credit Ledger → date range 01/04–31/03 → one file per ledger; (2) GSTR-9 annual return (CA files by December) — every figure consolidated; (3) Returns Dashboard → select FY → all 12 filings listed for click-through.
CA quotes me only the CGST figure — what do I post?▼
Double it. CGST always equals SGST for your intra-state transactions, and your combined heads hold both together. CGST ₹153.10 → post ₹306.19 combined (use the return's exact combined figure when paise differ due to rounding).
A bill has IGST on it — what changes?▼
Booking: nothing changes. The IGST amount joins the same SA GST I/P head (Bank still keeps only half). Reading the return: everything changes for that month — IGST makes CGST ≠ SGST in the payment table, so the "double the CGST" shortcut breaks. Copy the return's exact figures instead. This exact omission (an IGST-blind spreadsheet) miscounted ₹7,196 of Bank ITC in FY 2025-26 — IGST shows up on out-of-state bills like software licences and some insurance.
