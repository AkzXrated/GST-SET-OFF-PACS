Essential Adjustment to Rule 38 (Bank GSTIN)

Cooperative banks operate under restriction: they can only claim 50% of monthly Input Tax Credit (ITC). The remaining 50% must be booked straight as an operating expense at month-end. Meanwhile, Neethy Medicals and Fertilizer Depot are retail traders and can claim 100% of their ITC.

Audit Review (FY 2025-26 Performance)

You laid a solid foundation, which is commendable for a team operating without dedicated in-house tax advisors:

  • Systematic Monthly Data: Monthly sales and purchases were logged without fail in the primary ledger system.
  • RCM Compliance Concept: You recognized that rent paid to an unregistered landlord triggers RCM liability, and tried to record it in your accounts.
  • Challan Archiving: Tax payment challan receipts generated by the CA were correctly preserved and paid on time, avoiding interest penalties.
  • CA Colleague: The CA's computations on the portal filings (GSTR-3B) were structurally correct, providing a reliable ground truth to check against.

We identified major gaps in how books talk to portal returns, causing ledger balances to swell artificially:

  • The "Growing Balances" Ledger Loop: Because no monthly "set-off" entries were passed in the banking software, SA GST Input and SL GST Output balances grew indefinitely without ever resetting to zero.
  • Disconnected Tax Expense: Payments to the government were written off directly to a temporary head GST Paid to Govt, rather than being used to debit and clear the actual outstanding liability under SL GST Output.
  • Bank ITC Overstatement (Rule 38): The bank logged 100% of purchase ITC as an asset in the books, instead of writing off the ineligible 50% to expenses. This inflated the books asset ledger by ₹80,952.81.
  • Omitted Fertilizer Depot Inputs: Fertilizer Depot purchases were entirely omitted in the books, recording only RCM entries (Book Input: ₹20,100.37 vs GSTR-3B Input: ₹30,663.38).
  • Duplicate Challan PDF Storage: Neethy Medicals' folder contained duplicate files belonging to the Bank, causing tracking confusion.

To fix these gaps forever, we must implement three core changes in your ledger routing:

  • Link Payments to Liability: Stop closing tax payments directly against "GST Paid to Govt". Instead, route payments through `SL GST Output` first to neutralize the liability.
  • Perform Monthly Set-offs: Every month, when the CA provides the GSTR-3B filing data, pass a journal entry that offsets your `SL GST Output` liability using your available `SA GST Input` credit.
  • Divide Bank ITC 50:50: Implement the 50% permanent write-off rule for the Bank's purchases. When recording bank expenses, route 50% of the invoice GST directly to an expense ledger (e.g. `GST Ineligible ITC Expense`).

Monthly Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)

Follow this sequence strictly every month to avoid cumulative reconciliation errors at year-end. This aligns books with portal filings step-by-step.

Day 1 - 5

Invoice Logging & Rent Booking

Record all regular vendor purchases in the software. For the rent, post the monthly accrual voucher debiting RCM Input tax and crediting RCM Output tax heads. For the Bank, make sure 50% of purchase GST goes directly to the purchase cost or expense account.

Day 10 - 12

CA Return Drafting

Your CA compiles tax outputs (GSTR-1) and downloads GSTR-2B. Provide the CA with your raw books purchase data so they can claim any missed invoices on the portal, matching GSTR-2B exactly.

Day 15 - 18

Challan Payment & Entry

CA generates the GSTR-3B challan. Pay the amount via bank account and immediately post:
Dr. SL GST Output Tax (CGST+SGST)
Cr. Bank Account.
Do not park payments in "GST Paid to Govt" without linking them!

Day 19 - 20

Monthly Set-Off Posting

Obtain GSTR-3B summary from the CA. Post the monthly offsetting entry in books to absorb your SL GST Output against eligible SA GST Input credits utilized. Your output ledger for that month should now fall to zero.

GST Glossary Decoded for Beginners

ITC (Input Tax Credit)
The tax paid by you on purchases. Think of it as a pre-paid coupon or digital asset balance in the portal that you can use to pay off your own output tax liabilities.
Outward GST (Output Tax)
The GST you collect from customers on sales. It is a pure liability; you are just a collector holding the money on behalf of the government until return filing.
RCM (Reverse Charge)
A rule where the buyer pays tax directly to the government instead of the seller. Required when renting business premises from an unregistered landlord.
GSTR-3B
The final monthly summary return filed to the government showing total outward sales, eligible ITC claimed, and the final net cash paid via bank challan.
GSTR-2B
An auto-generated, read-only statement from the portal showing every invoice uploaded by your suppliers. If a purchase bill isn't in GSTR-2B, you cannot claim its ITC.
Rule 38 (Banking ITC)
A tax restriction stating that banks can only claim 50% of monthly input credits because their interest income is exempt from GST. The other 50% is lost forever and must be expensed.
